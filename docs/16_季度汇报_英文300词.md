This quarter had a literature and design stage, then a first working prototype.

- **Literature and design.** I read on personalised learning and learner modelling, the ICAP framework, meta-analyses and reviews of intelligent tutoring systems, and hybrid human-AI teaching. Three assumptions behind my earlier plan were corrected: personalisation should be data-driven rather than student-selected, static grouping is insufficient, and research without a reliable measure cannot proceed. Evaluation in this field proved inconsistent, many systems being judged by self-made tests rather than standardised ones. The stage produced a student-profile design — three achievement emotions after Pekrun, an Active/Passive split of ICAP because the platform has no free-text input, and diagnostic classification modelling as the foundation — plus a staged plan.

- **Prototype system.** A Python/Flask application that retrieves courseware pages and generates explanations with DeepSeek, running two versions side by side for blind comparison, where only the prompts differ, so a difference is attributable to the prompt rather than to retrieval or the model. Chapter 1 of Rosen's Discrete Mathematics serves as the test dataset.

- **Evaluation.** Rather than writing my own rubric, I adopted the Mathematical Quality of Instruction scheme. Four rounds of blind scoring give the current version 2.45 against a 2.30 baseline. In a cold-start comparison with identical courseware and questions, it scores 2.65 against 1.25 for a bare model.

- **Knowledge structure.** 114 nodes, 207 subgoal labels and 268 misconceptions for Chapter 1, with every quoted step verified against the courseware by script.

- **Outstanding work.** The profile remains a design: the platform logs only solving and explaining. Results cover one chapter and one rater.
