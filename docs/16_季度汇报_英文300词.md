This quarter I built and evaluated the first working version of the explanation system.

- **System.** A Python/Flask application that retrieves the relevant courseware pages and generates explanations with DeepSeek. It runs two versions side by side for blind A/B comparison: the version is assigned randomly, the label is hidden until both answers are rated, and only the prompts differ, so any difference is attributable to the prompt rather than to retrieval or the model. I brought in three earlier course materials, then selected Chapter 1 of Rosen's Discrete Mathematics as the primary dataset and reused it in later rounds.

- **Evaluation.** I searched for an existing instrument instead of writing my own, and adopted the Mathematical Quality of Instruction coding scheme, using its Explanations, Sense-Making, Content Errors, Imprecision and Clarity codes. Four rounds of model-based blind scoring have finished; the current optimized version scores 2.45 against a 2.30 baseline, but the gap is narrow and the baseline was also lifted, so I report it as a trend rather than a proven gain.

- **Knowledge structure (main milestone).** A minimal structure for Chapter 1: 114 nodes, 207 subgoal labels and 268 common misconceptions. Because a language model wrote the drafts, I added a source-grounding check that rejects any quoted step not found verbatim in the courseware, plus negative tests. Selected nodes are injected into the prompt, and on a real generation I confirmed the model organised its explanation around the injected steps.

- **Also completed.** Page-level ingestion of the textbooks, rendering of mathematical notation, and one cold-start comparison: with identical courseware and questions, the optimized version scores 2.65 against 1.25 for a bare model.

- **Limitations.** Minor content errors still appear; output length is poorly controlled; results rely on one rater and an unvalidated mapping, so cross-chapter validation remains ahead.
